KWESE DECODER TECHNICIAN GUIDE: FTA BYPASS & SERIAL RECOVERY
===============================================================================

This guide outlines both the non-destructive software bypass method and the 
low-level hardware flashing procedure for repurposing Kwesé TV DVB-S2 decoders 
into standard Free-To-Air (FTA) satellite receivers.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THE ETHERNET PORT CANNOT BE USED WITH PuTTY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* No SSH/Telnet Server: The factory firmware running on Kwesé decoders does not 
  host an active SSH or Telnet daemon listening on the RJ45 Ethernet port.
* No Remote Management: Network protocols are strictly bound to secure content 
  delivery platforms and internal system metrics.
* Conclusion: You CANNOT use an Ethernet cable paired with PuTTY to access the 
  command console or flash firmware. Low-level access requires a direct physical 
  serial connection to the motherboard's microcontroller lines.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METHOD A: THE SOFTWARE-BASED INSTALLER BYPASS (RECOMMENDED FIRST STEP)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This method forces the decoder's native factory OS to open its manual/blind scan 
transponder tools, circumventing the locked Kwesé activation wall.

Step 1: Setup Infrastructure
   * Connect a working satellite dish coax cable to the 'LNB IN' port.
   * Point the dish toward an active FTA satellite operating in your region 
     (e.g., Eutelsat 7B/7C, Astra 2F, Intelsat 20).
   * Connect HDMI or AV to your test monitor and power on the box.

Step 2: Access System Settings
   * Press 'Menu' on the remote control.
   * Navigate to 'System Settings'.
   * Scroll down to highlight 'Time Settings' (DO NOT click OK; just hover over it).

Step 3: Trigger the Hidden Engineering Menu
   * Press the GREEN button on the remote control exactly once.
   * Immediately type '4362' on the numpad.
   * Press the MUTE button immediately after.

Step 4: Execute the Scan
   * If successful, the native technician's manual configuration layout will appear.
   * Select 'Manual Scan' or 'Blind Scan'.
   * Match your LNB Frequency parameters (typically 9750/10600 for Universal LNBs).
   * Run the scan to extract and save all unencrypted channels directly to storage.

*Tech Note (Unactivated Box Loop): If the unit is fresh out of the box and stuck 
at a mandatory first-time installation configuration, select 'Ignore' and try the 
technician override bypass PIN: '1111'.

3. METHOD B: HARDWARE SERIAL RECOVERY VIA PuTTY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use this physical configuration if the firmware revision on the board actively 
strips or blocks remote control shortcuts.

Step 1: Probe the UART Bus
   * Strip down the external plastic casing to reveal the mainboard PCB.
   * Locate an unpopulated row of 3 or 4 solder pin holes near the main SoC processor.
   * They are typically marked: VCC (3.3V/5V), RX, TX, GND.
   * CRITICAL SECURITY WARNING: NEVER hook up the VCC line to your adapter. Connecting 
     external power here can cross-circuit and fry both the decoder chip and your PC.

Step 2: Align the Bus Connections
   * Procure a USB-to-TTL Serial Debugging Adapter (CP2102, PL2303, or FTDI chipsets).
   * Cross-wire the communication channels:
     - Adapter GND ---> Decoder Board GND
     - Adapter TX  ---> Decoder Board RX
     - Adapter RX  ---> Decoder Board TX

Step 3: Map the Windows Comms Interface
   * Connect the USB side to your computer.
   * Launch Windows Device Manager (`devmgmt.msc`) and look under 'Ports (COM & LPT)' 
     to confirm your interface identifier (e.g., COM3).

Step 4: Launch and Tune PuTTY
   * Initialize a new PuTTY session and define the following low-level params:
     - Connection Type: Serial
     - Serial Line: [Input your matched COM Port]
     - Speed (Baud Rate): 115200 (Default standard for STB bootloaders)
     - Data bits: 8
     - Stop bits: 1
     - Parity: None
     - Flow Control: None

Step 5: Catch the Boot Execution Trace
   * Hit 'Open' to fire up the raw terminal interface window.
   * Supply main AC/DC power to the Kwesé decoder unit.
   * Scrolling hex strings and kernel initialization flags should fill the screen.
   * Intercept Bootloader: Tap execution escape triggers (such as Spacebar, Esc, 
     or Ctrl+C continuously) within the first 1.5 seconds of boot-up to intercept 
     the sequence and enter the interactive U-Boot/system shell terminal prompt.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CHIPSET & FIRMWARE CONSIDERATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* SoC Identification: Desolder or view the main processing core. Kwesé boxes 
  traditionally deploy Ali architectures (e.g., M3510/M3511 series) or NationalChip 
  (GX) frameworks.
* Always Backup First: Do not deploy an untested image immediately. Run chip dump 
  utilities (like Ali Flash Loader or GXDownloader) via your UART adapter first 
  to execute a complete full-flash dump backup (.bin file) of your native memory.
* Source Material: Generic replacement firmware images must explicitly match the 
  processor model designation and the exact physical motherboard structural revision ID.
